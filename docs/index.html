Introduction

Understanding how crime is distributed across Los Angeles is essential for identifying patterns that can inform public safety strategies, law enforcement resource allocation, and community awareness efforts. Los Angeles is a large and diverse city, with varying socioeconomic, demographic, and geographic characteristics that may influence the frequency and type of crimes occurring in different areas. By analyzing the distribution of crimes — such as theft, burglary, identity theft, and assault — we can uncover whether certain neighborhoods or population groups are more vulnerable to specific types of crime. This analysis can help policymakers and local authorities develop targeted interventions to reduce crime rates, improve safety, and promote equity in public protection across the city.

Detailed Column and Data Info

The dataset we are analyzing covers crime data from 2020 to present day in the city of Los Angeles and was taken from data.gov, The Home of the U.S. Government's Open Data. The dataset contains 1,004,992 rows and 28 columns, including the following features: division of records file number, date reported, date occurred, time occurred, area, area name, four digit area code, type of crime committed, crime code, MO codes, victim age, victim sex, victim race, and crime premises.

Visualizations

Visualization 1 - Bar Graphs Showing Most Common Crimes Per Year (2020-2024)

Takeaway:This visualization provides a broad foundation by showing which crimes occur most frequently each year. It highlights persistent, high-volume offenses such as theft and burglary, showing how certain crime types consistently dominate the landscape. This overall trend helps frame the more detailed demographic, geographic, and behavioral analyses that follow.

Static Data Visualization:This visualization uses a consistent color legend that allows viewers to quickly track individual crime types across all four years. The uniform scaling and clear labeling make it easy to compare proportions visually without needing additional interaction.

Visualization 2 - Bar Graphs Showing Proportion of Crimes Against Victim Demographics by Race (2020–2024)

Takeaway:Building on the crime totals from Visualization 1, this figure examines which racial groups are most affected by these common crimes. It reveals clear disparities in victimization, suggesting that certain demographic groups face disproportionately higher rates of specific crimes. This shifts the analysis from “what types of crime occur?” to “who is most impacted by them?”

Static Data Visualization:This chart relies on visual comparison across the grid layout. The consistent color coding for each demographic group serves as a visual guide that helps users track changes in victimization patterns across the four-year period.

Visualization 3 - Interactive Altair Histogram Showing Distribution of Victim Age by Crime Type

Takeaway:After examining racial demographics, this interactive histogram focuses on age-related vulnerability. It shows which crime types disproportionately affect children, teens, working-age adults, or older individuals. This adds another layer of insight by connecting crime trends to the life stages of victims, revealing that some crimes cluster around younger populations while others disproportionately impact older adults.

Interactive Data Visualization:Users can select specific crime types from a dropdown menu to filter the histogram, allowing focused exploration of how individual crimes affect different age groups. The "All" option shows the complete distribution. Additionally, Altair's built-in zoom and pan features let users examine specific age ranges in detail. Hovering over bars reveals tooltips with exact crime counts and age group information.

Visualization 4 - Interactive Altair Map of Crime Type Across Los Angeles (2020-2023)

Takeaway:With demographic patterns established, this map visualizes how crimes are distributed geographically across the city. It shows that crimes are not evenly spread; instead, certain neighborhoods experience higher concentrations of specific offenses. This spatial perspective connects earlier demographic findings with place-based realities, suggesting that risk levels are shaped not only by who victims are but also by where they live.

Interactive Data Visualization:A radio button selector allows users to switch between individual years (2020-2023) or view aggregate data across all years. This temporal filtering reveals whether crime hotspots remain consistent or shift over time. Hovering over any district displays a tooltip showing the district name and exact crime count, providing precise information without cluttering the map.

Visualization 5 - D3 Scatter Plot Showing Crime Type in Relation to Victim Age vs Time of Day

Takeaway:The final visualization pairs victim age with time-of-day patterns to show when people of different age groups face the highest risk. It reveals behavioral and situational trends—for example, younger victims appearing in later-night incidents or older victims being more vulnerable during daytime hours. This completes the analysis by linking demographics and geography to temporal patterns of crime.

Interactive Data Visualization:Users can filter the scatter plot by crime type to isolate specific patterns and identify when particular offenses cluster by time and victim age. D3's zoom and pan functionality allows detailed exploration of specific age ranges or time windows. Interactive tooltips appear on hover, displaying exact victim age, time of incident, and crime type, enabling users to examine individual data points while maintaining the overall pattern view.

This interactive scatter plot shows the relationship between victim age and the time of day at which crimes occurred in Los Angeles from 2020 to 2025. Users can filter the visualization by crime type using the dropdown menu and adjust the displayed time window with the range sliders. These controls make it easier to explore when different crimes occur and how victimization varies across age groups. Hovering over any point reveals additional details about the incident, including date and weapon information when available.
